Measuring guide

How to measure your kitchen or wardrobe.

All you need is a tape measure, a pen and your phone. Ten minutes of measuring lets you design your space in our free 3D designer and gets you a more accurate quote.

Kitchens

Measuring your kitchen.

Work around the room one wall at a time and write everything down in millimetres.

  1. Draw a rough plan. A simple sketch from above is fine. Mark each wall, plus doors, windows and anything that sticks out, like bulkheads or columns.
  2. Measure each wall. Measure in millimetres from corner to corner, once at bench height and once near the ceiling. Walls are rarely perfectly square, so write down both numbers.
  3. Measure the ceiling height. Check it in two or three places. This tells us how tall your overhead cupboards and pantry can be.
  4. Mark doors and windows. Measure from the corner to where each one starts, then its width and height. For windows, add the sill height from the floor.
  5. Mark plumbing, gas and power. Note where the sink water and waste pipes, the gas point and the power points are. Moving them is possible, but it affects cost.
  6. List your appliances. Write down the fridge cavity you need (width and height), and the sizes of any oven, cooktop, rangehood or dishwasher you are keeping.
  7. Take photos. One photo of each wall, standing back as far as you can, plus close-ups of pipes and power points.

Wardrobes

Measuring your wardrobe.

The same idea for built-in robes and walk-ins. Measure in a few places, because floors and ceilings are rarely level.

  1. Measure the width. Measure wall to wall at the top, middle and bottom, and use the smallest number.
  2. Measure the height. Floor to ceiling at both ends and in the middle. Note any cornice or bulkhead.
  3. Check the depth you have. Hanging clothes need about 600 mm of depth. If the space is shallower, tell us and we will plan shelves and drawers instead.
  4. Mark what's on the walls. Light switches, power points, air-con vents, skirting boards and windows all affect the layout.
  5. For walk-in robes, measure the room. Measure every wall, mark where the door is and which way it opens, and note any windows.
  6. Take photos. Photos of the space and of your current wardrobe help us plan how much hanging and drawer space you need.

FAQ

Good questions.

Do my measurements need to be exact?

No. Your measurements help us and our 3D designer give you a realistic first design and rough idea of cost. Before anything is made, we measure your space properly on site.

Should I measure in centimetres or millimetres?

Millimetres. That is how cabinet makers work, and it avoids mix-ups. 2.4 metres is 2400 mm.

What if my walls aren't straight or square?

That is normal, especially in older homes. Measure each wall at a few heights and note the smallest number. We allow for it with scribe and filler panels when we build.
