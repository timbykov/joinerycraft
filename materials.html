Who we buy from

Our suppliers.

Every board and benchtop we fit comes from one of four Australian suppliers. Here's a little about each one, with photos of their finishes. Tap through to see their full ranges, or come and see real samples in our showroom.

Boards, doors and panels

Polytec

Australian made and owned, Polytec was started on the NSW Central Coast more than 30 years ago and is still made there today. Their boards come in realistic timber and stone looks, matte and gloss solids, and ready-made door profiles like Ascot and Hampton.

We use it for: Most of our kitchens, wardrobes and laundries.

See Polytec colours ↗ Polytec photo gallery ↗

Boards and laminates

Laminex

Laminex started in a tin shed in suburban Melbourne in 1934 and now makes its boards and laminates in seven plants across four states. Its colour collection covers soft whites, deep solids and Australian timber looks like Tasmanian Oak and Spotted Gum.

We use it for: Timber-look cabinetry, wardrobes and hard-wearing laminate benchtops.

See Laminex colours ↗

Stone benchtops

Caesarstone

Caesarstone has made premium surfaces since 1987 and is sold in more than 50 countries. Its current Australian range is crystalline silica free, in line with the 2024 ban on engineered stone.

We use it for: Kitchen benchtops, islands with waterfall ends, splashbacks and vanities.

See Caesarstone colours ↗

Stone benchtops, by WK Stone

Quantum Quartz

Quantum Quartz is made by WK Stone, an Australian-owned company since 1989. Its Quantum Zero range is made from recycled materials with zero crystalline silica, in marble looks, concretes and solid whites.

We use it for: Benchtops and splashbacks when you want a marble look on a tighter budget.

FAQ

Good questions.

Should I choose polyurethane or board doors?

Polyurethane (2-pac) doors are spray painted in almost any Dulux colour and give a smooth, seamless finish. Decorative boards from Polytec and Laminex are hard-wearing and come in realistic woodgrains, stone looks and solid colours. Many kitchens use both, for example board carcasses with painted doors. We'll help you choose at the measure-up.

What's the difference between stone and laminate benchtops?

Engineered stone from Caesarstone or Quantum Quartz is heavy, hard-wearing and can have thick edges and waterfall ends. Laminate costs less and still looks good, which makes it popular for laundries, rentals and budget-conscious kitchens.

Can I see samples before I decide?

Yes. Our Chipping Norton showroom has stone slabs, board colours and Blum hardware to look at and touch. Visits are by appointment.

Can you match an existing colour?

Usually, yes. Painted polyurethane can be colour-matched, and we can often find a close board match. Bring a sample or a photo to the showroom.
